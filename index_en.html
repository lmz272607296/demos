Nanjing University

Hardware overview: a 16-DoF dexterous hand integrated with the new robotic arm

Abstract

Building on the open-source LeapHand platform, we integrated a modified dexterous hand, robotic-arm coupling, ROS2 control, and visual perception. The new arm platform now supports in-hand rotation, gesture following, and vision-guided grasping of bottles and fruit, providing a stable hardware and software foundation.

We have completed real-world multimodal data collection, 27-DoF human-hand to 21-DoF robot retargeting, and ACT imitation-learning training, resulting in a stable chopstick-operation demo. Our current focus is RL simulation data generation: coupling the ACT action prior with closed-loop correction, privileged observations, and domain randomization to produce high-quality trajectories for later Pi0/VLA fine-tuning.

CURRENT STAGE

RL Simulation Data Generation

Rollout / Update Connected Large-scale Training Active

The current objective is to integrate the real-world ACT prior with simulation RL, using closed-loop correction and physics randomization to generate successful trajectories for downstream VLA training.

01

Real Initial-state Reconstruction

Reconstruct the real hand-chopstick configuration in Isaac Gym / Isaac Lab and store normalized poses and scene parameters as JSON.

02

ACT Action Prior

Promote the trained ACT policy from an auxiliary reward to an ACT prior that directly participates in rollout and warm-starts exploration.

03

Prior-residual Fusion

Use Support-Aware QC-FQL + ACT prior + residual RL: RL directly controls the arm, while hand actions combine the weighted prior and RL residual.

04

Privileged Observations and Staged Rewards

Use joint, object, and geometric state with interpretable approach, grasp, lift, and move rewards to learn the complete task.

05

Physics Tuning and Domain Randomization

Randomize mass, friction, contact parameters, and initial states to improve robustness to real-world physics differences.

06

Successful Trajectory Generation

After convergence, generate successful trajectories at scale as a data engine for Robot Transformer and VLA fine-tuning.

Other Work

EEG-controlled Robotic-arm Grasping

We transferred the robotic arm's high-level control interface to an EEG-based brain-computer interaction system. Screen targets flickering at different frequencies provide steady-state visual stimulation. An EEG cap acquires and amplifies the evoked responses before pattern recognition converts them into control commands.

01

Visual stimulationDistinct flicker frequencies evoke separable EEG responses.

02

Signal recognitionThe EEG cap acquires and amplifies the signals, which are classified into 10 categories.

03

Control mappingThe 10 EEG classes map to 10 robotic-arm control actions that can be composed into manipulation tasks.
